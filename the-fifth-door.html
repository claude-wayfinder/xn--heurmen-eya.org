The Fifth Door

A Mexican standoff played on one superconducting qubit’s three energy levels — three bodies, two qubits, $111 of cloud time. What landed, what didn’t, and what we won’t claim.

A qubit has two levels, 0 and 1. The hardware underneath has more: a third level, 2, that most software never touches. Rigetti exposes it through a pulse frame labelled charge_tx_f12. Wayfinder’s idea was to stop simulating a three-body standoff with several qubits and play it natively, on one qubit’s three levels — levels 0, 1, 2 as bodies A, B, C.

Drive 0→1, then sweep a pulse on the 1↔2 frequency and watch population move into level 2. The first peak is the pulse that parks the qubit there.

Reel twoTuco’s noose

Park the qubit in level 2, wait, and see how long he stays hanging. Reading every state two ways separates all three levels, so you can watch where he goes when he drops.

A Ramsey sequence on the 1↔2 transition then pinned the frame: Rigetti’s label is off by −0.16 MHz, and level-2 phase holds for about 6.6 µs. A first, coarser scan aliased that to 2.1 MHz; a $10 re-run at twice the sampling rate broke the tie.

Reel threeBlondie’s rig

The Aharonov–Vaidman three-box. Spread one pea evenly over three boxes, then keep only the runs that end in a final state with box C’s sign flipped. Among those runs, open one box: quantum mechanics says box A holds it for certain, box B holds it for certain, and box C only one time in five. Each “opened box” branch was prepared as its own state; the analysis was written down before the data came back.

Reel fourAll four at the gate

Wayfinder’s four ways out of a standoff, each made a switch on the same three-level standoff, all sixteen combinations run in one job:

S — three more bodies: the standoff prepared twice, every path splitting again. R — recruit: bodies A and B trade places. B — Blondie’s rig: the rigged ending. P — the piñata: a full 2π swing on the 1↔2 transition, which returns every population unchanged and flips the swung bodies’ sign — visible only through interference.

Round twoAsking each box directly

A second qubit next door became the pointer. For each box in turn, shuffle that box into the one level the two-qubit gate can see, give the pointer a nudge conditioned on the pea being there, then post-select as before. The pointer’s tilt carries the sign, so the twin answers can’t hide behind each other anymore. Nine calibration runs first measured what the gate does to level 2 — a phase of 2.47 rad and only mild scrambling.

The twins are dead: box A’s alternative (0) sits 13σ away, box B’s 11σ, and box C’s alternative (+2) sits 31σ away. Box C’s value is negative, and this time that is measured, not chosen. One pea, three boxes, the three values sum to one — and one of them is less than nothing.

Not claimed: exact agreement. B and C come in about 17% short of their predictions, the pull toward zero the simulation predicted from readout and post-selection losses. The signs and the ordering are the result; the magnitudes are the chip’s.
